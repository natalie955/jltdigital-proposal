Why This Way

Spread the Payments,
Not the Build.

There are two ways to make a R45,600 project fit a R2,000 month. Only one of them actually helps.

Stretch the build — the slow road

Deliver four hours a month until the scope is done. The monthly figure works, but the system takes the best part of two years to arrive. Every week without automated follow-up is another week of leads going cold — and the problem you are paying to solve keeps costing you while you pay to solve it.

Stretch the payments — this proposal

Build it properly in six to eight weeks, exactly as originally scoped, and settle the cost over eighteen months. The follow-up automation starts recovering leads from month two — while you are still paying for it, rather than long after.

The arithmetic that matters. Two hundred and thirty leads came in over three months and eight converted. Whatever the automated follow-up recovers — even a handful of jobs — it does so from month two under this plan, rather than from month nine or later under a stretched build. For a business quoting CNC, signage and dye sub work, a single recovered job of any size covers several months of the instalment.

Terms

How the Plan Works

Stated plainly, because a payment plan with vague terms is worse than no payment plan.

Signed agreement, debit order

The plan is set out in a short written agreement with the schedule attached, and instalments run by debit order on a fixed date each month. No invoicing back and forth, no chasing, no ambiguity about what is owed when.

You own it from day one

Everything is built inside the client's own GoHighLevel account, so the workflows are theirs the moment they go live — not held hostage until the final instalment clears. We are not in the business of building something we could take away.

If an instalment is missed

We call you before anything else happens. Support and any further work pause until the schedule is back on track, but nothing is switched off or deleted — the system keeps running. We would rather rework a schedule than lose a client over one difficult month.

What this plan is, honestly. It is us carrying the cost of the build for eighteen months so the client does not have to. There is no interest and no premium, which means the plan only works on the basis of a signed agreement and a debit order — not because we expect trouble, but because that is the arrangement that lets us offer it without adding a financing charge that would make it more expensive for everyone.
