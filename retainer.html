The Shift

Same System. Different Pace.

You've asked for a monthly retainer rather than a single fee. That's a fair ask from a client who's been burned before — so here's exactly what changes, and what deliberately doesn't.

Scope stays whole

All 76 hours. Both sales flows, the Recurring Clients board, opt-out tagging, Abandoned/Lost routing, templates, QA and training. Nothing is quietly dropped to hit a monthly number.

The rate comes down

R500/hr instead of R600. Predictable monthly revenue is worth something to us, and we'd rather pass that back than pad the hours. Total lands at R38,000 rather than R45,600.

The timeline stretches

This is the real trade-off and we won't dress it up: roughly 19 months instead of 8 weeks. The system arrives in working stages rather than all at once.

Training gets better

Genuinely. Instead of two training days at the end, the team learns one module at a time as it goes live — which is how people actually retain a system they were previously abandoned with.

They can stop

Two months' notice, any time. Everything built to that point is finished, documented and running on its own — never a half-wired flow nobody can use.

Same scope, lower rate, longer runway — and never locked in.
How It Works

Billed Monthly.
Built in Quarters.

R2,000 a month buys four hours. Four hours isn't enough to finish anything meaningful — most of it goes on remembering where you left off, and the account sits half-configured in between.

So we bank the hours and work in quarterly sprints of twelve. You pay the same R2,000 every month; we deliver a complete, tested, trained module every three. Nothing is ever left mid-build on a live system.

What Lands, and When

Sequenced hardest-hitting first. CNC & Signage carries the biggest leak, so it goes live before anything tidier gets touched.

Why this order. CNC and Signage is where the money is leaking — open-text intake means those leads stall waiting on back-and-forth, and they're the ones going quiet. If you'd rather resequence, the order is yours to set — the hours don't change.
Investment

Pick Your Pace

All three cost exactly the same in total. The only thing that changes is how long they wait for a system that's losing them leads every week it isn't there.

R38,000 total, whichever you choose. Same 76 hours, same R500/hr, same deliverables — simply paid faster or slower.
Three ways to run it
R2,000/mo
Steady — ~19 months

4 hours a month, delivered as a 12-hour sprint each quarter. What you've asked for, and it works — it's simply the longest road to a finished system.

R4,000/mo
Balanced — ~10 months

8 hours a month. Both sales flows live inside the first year and the whole system done in under one. This is the one we'd point you at if the budget can stretch.

R6,000/mo
Fast — ~7 months

12 hours a month. Closest to the original build timeline while still spreading the cost, with CNC & Signage live in roughly three months.

You can change gear at any time

Start at R2,000 and move up when cash allows, or buy a one-off block of hours at R500/hr to pull a specific sprint forward — useful if a busy season is coming and you want the follow-up automation live before it hits. Moving up takes effect immediately; moving down applies at the end of the current sprint, so nothing is left half-finished.

Every hour is logged

Hours are tracked against each sprint and shown on the invoice, so you always know exactly what the retainer bought. Unused hours roll forward into the next sprint — never lost, never quietly kept.

What isn't included. GoHighLevel platform and reseller fees, WhatsApp's per-sub-account charge of around $10 a month, and Meta's per-message costs outside the 24-hour reply window all sit on the client's own account at the vendor's price. We never resell them or take a margin. Xero stays manual as requested, ad and campaign work stays with you, and travel to Pretoria is at no charge.
Why This Suits Them

The Slow Version Trains Better

This is the part worth reading twice. For a team that was sold a system and left to figure it out, monthly beats big-bang.

Six sessions, not two days

Every sprint ends with a working session on what just went live. By handover the team has been trained six separate times on six manageable pieces — instead of drinking from a firehose for two days and forgetting most of it by the following week.

They see it working before committing further

By roughly month nine the CNC & Signage flow is live and measurable. If it isn't moving the numbers, they've spent R18,000 and can stop — rather than having committed the full amount to a promise on a page.

Nothing is ever left half-built

Each sprint closes with a module that is finished, tested, documented and independent. Stop after any sprint and what's there keeps running — the exact opposite of what happened to them last time.
